### A little more about the featured projects

#### ⌑ Ryana 
Ryana is built around the idea that learning to program involves more than collecting finished solutions. It provides students with a place to record snippets, errors, observations, and progress — creating a personal programming knowledge base.

#### ⌑ Pathfinder
Pathfinder explores how AI can be approached as something to understand rather than simply consume. It provides a roadmap for parents and students navigating the rapidly changing landscape of AI and education.

#### ⌑ ZetteNote
ZetteNote applies ideas from the Zettelkasten method to student learning, encouraging notes to become connected pieces of knowledge rather than isolated documents.

#### ⌑ ASCII Box Drawing
A practical reference for one of my favourite areas of digital typography: using ASCII and Unicode characters to create diagrams, interfaces, documentation, and visual structures directly in text.

## ∴ How I Approach Development

My projects tend to follow a few recurring principles:

| Principle | In practice |
| --- | --- |
| **01 · Modular Thinking** | Break large problems into smaller, understandable components. |
| **02 · Clarity** | Prefer readable code and visible structure over unnecessary cleverness. |
| **03 · Incremental Complexity** | Start with a simple working idea, then introduce complexity deliberately. |
| **04 · Reproducibility** | Make projects understandable, repeatable, and maintainable by someone other than their original author. |
| **05 · Learning Through Making** | Use software as both a solution and a way of exploring the problem itself. |

## ⊕ Currently Exploring

This is perhaps the most important section for a personal GitHub profile.

Rather than treating the README as a catalogue of finished work, I want it to reflect **what I am currently thinking about and building**.

At the moment, my interests sit around the intersection of:
- **AI & education** — exploring how AI can support learning without replacing the thinking that makes learning valuable.
- **Developer tools** — building small, focused tools that remove friction from programming and documentation.
- **Learning systems** — experimenting with ways to capture, organise, connect, and revisit knowledge.
- **Human-centred software** — designing tools around the needs of learners rather than simply the capabilities of technology.
- **Text as interface** — continuing to explore Markdown, ASCII, Unicode, diagrams, and other ways of communicating structure through plain text.
- **Creative technology** — finding interesting places where programming, design, education, and experimentation overlap.
